Chronology of Hampi

3rd Century BC

Hampi was part of the Ashokan Empire, known for its strategic location near the river.

1336 AD

Foundation of the Vijayanagara Empire by Harihara I and Bukka Raya I on the south bank of Tungabhadra.

1509 - 1529 AD

The Golden Era under Krishnadevaraya. Most grand structures and international trade boomed during this time.

1565 AD

Battle of Talikota. The empire was defeated by the Deccan Sultanates, leading to the destruction of the city.

1800 AD

Colin Mackenzie, the first Surveyor General of India, "discovers" and starts documenting the ruins.

1986 AD

UNESCO declares Hampi as a World Heritage Site, recognizing its architectural and historical significance.

The Sacred Temples

Virupaksha Temple

Dedicated to Lord Virupaksha, a form of Shiva, this temple predates the empire itself. Its 160-foot gopuram dominates the skyline. Visitors can see the inverted shadow of the gopuram through a small hole in the wall, a marvel of ancient engineering.

Vittala Temple

The crown jewel of Hampi. It houses the iconic Stone Chariot, which is actually a shrine. The Ranga Mantapa is famous for its 56 musical pillars that produce melodic notes when tapped gently. This complex represents the zenith of Dravidian architecture.

Krishna Temple

Built by King Krishnadevaraya in 1513 to celebrate the conquest of Udayagiri. The temple is unique for its carvings of sub-deities and the Balakrishna image that was once housed here (now in a Chennai museum).

Hazara Rama Temple

Considered the private chapel of the Vijayanagara kings. The name "Hazara Rama" (Thousand Ramas) refers to the thousands of carvings on the walls depicting the story of the Ramayana in sequential panels.

Badavi Linga

Commissioned by a poor peasant woman, this 3-meter high monolithic Linga stands in a chamber that is permanently flooded by a canal. It symbolizes the omnipresence of Shiva in nature.

Sasivekalu & Kadalekalu

Two distinct monolithic Ganesha statues. Sasivekalu (Mustard Seed) is carved from a single boulder, while Kadalekalu (Gram Seed) sits in a temple with tall pillars offering a panoramic view of Hampi Bazar.

The Royal Enclosure

Lotus Mahal

A fine example of Indo-Islamic fusion architecture. It was used as a council chamber for royal women. The structure was ingeniously cooled by water pipes running through the walls, an early form of air conditioning.

Elephant Stables

Consisting of 11 domed chambers, these stables housed the royal elephants. The design reflects Persian influence and the wealth of the empire which could afford such grand quarters for animals.

Queen's Bath

A rectangular building with a plain exterior but a stunning interior. It features a sunken pool surrounded by corridors with ornate balconies and lotus-shaped fountains.

Mahanavami Dibba

A massive three-layered platform decorated with intricate carvings of elephants, horses, and dancers. It was the stage from which the King watched the grand festivals of Navaratri.

Stepped Tank (Pushkarani)

Discovered during excavations in the 1980s, this tank is constructed of black schist. Each stone is marked with a number or character, suggesting it was "pre-fabricated" and assembled on-site.

Underground Shiva Temple

Built below ground level, the inner sanctum is usually submerged in water. It is one of the oldest temples in the complex and was likely used for private royal ceremonies.
