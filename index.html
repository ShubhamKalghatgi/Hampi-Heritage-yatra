Hampī Itihāsa Anusandhāna

Journey through the sprawling ruins of the legendary Vijayanagara Empire

The Sacred Chronicles

Virupaksha Temple

Believed to have functioned uninterruptedly since the 7th century, this temple survived the 1565 destruction of Hampi. It is dedicated to Virupaksha, a form of Lord Shiva, and remains a vibrant center of worship today. The temple complex includes a massive 50-meter-high gopuram and a unique pinhole camera effect where the shadow of the main tower falls inverted on the wall of a small room behind the sanctum.

Main Feature: Known for its inverted shadow phenomenon and its survival as the only continuously active place of worship in Hampi.

Vittala Temple

This 15th-century masterpiece is the artistic soul of Hampi. It houses the world-famous Stone Chariot, which is actually a shrine for Garuda. The main hall, Maha Mandapa, is supported by 56 massive granite pillars that emit musical notes when tapped. These pillars were carved from single blocks of rock, each sounding a different note from the Indian musical scale.

Main Feature: Famous for the Iconic Stone Chariot and the Musical Pillars (SaReGaMa Pillars).

Achyutaraya Temple

Built in 1534 AD during the reign of Achyuta Deva Raya, this temple is located in a secluded valley between Matanga Hill and Gandhamadana Hill. It is one of the few temples that feature two concentric prakaras (courtyards). The temple is dedicated to Lord Tiruvengalanatha (Vishnu). The bazaar street in front of it was once a bustling center for the trade of precious gems and silk.

Main Feature: Its double-wall courtyard architecture and the highly detailed carvings on its massive pillars.

Hazara Rama Temple

Functioning as the private place of worship for the Kings and the Royal family, this temple is a stone-carved narrative. The exterior walls are decorated with five horizontal rows of friezes depicting elephants, horses, soldiers, and dancers. Inside, the pillars and walls are covered in thousands (Hazara) of detailed carvings from the Ramayana epic, essentially acting as a visual storybook for the royals.

Main Feature: The Bas-Relief Storytelling depicting the entire epic of Ramayana in stone.

Krishna Temple

Commissioned by Krishnadevaraya in 1513 AD to commemorate his military victory over Gajapati kings of Odisha. The temple is known for its elegant Yali (mythical creature) pillars and its sacred water tank (Pushkarani). It once housed a magnificent Balakrishna idol which is now preserved in the Chennai Museum.

Main Feature: The Victory Inscriptions and its unique Sub-shrines dedicated to different incarnations of Vishnu.

Badavi Linga

Carved out of a single giant boulder, this is the largest monolithic Shiva Linga in Hampi, standing at 3 meters tall. Legend says it was commissioned by a poor (Badava) peasant woman. The base of the Linga is permanently submerged in water, as a small canal from the Tungabhadra river passes through the sanctum, symbolizing the descent of the Ganga through Shiva's hair.

Main Feature: Being the largest monolithic Shiva Linga in Hampi, constantly submerged in water.

Sasivekalu Ganesha

Standing on the southern slope of Hemakuta Hill, this 8-foot-tall statue is carved from a single block of rock. The belly of the Ganesha is tied with a snake, which symbolizes the legend of Ganesha tying a snake around his waist after eating too much. "Sasivekalu" means mustard seed in Kannada, referring to the rounded shape of the idol.

Main Feature: The snake carved around the belly and its unique open pavilion architecture.

Kadalekalu Ganesha

Nearby the Sasivekalu statue, this Ganesha is much larger, standing at nearly 15 feet. It is one of the largest statues in South India. The name "Kadalekalu" translates to Bengal Gram, which the belly of the statue resembles. The temple hall is filled with slender, ornate pillars characteristic of the Vijayanagara style.

Main Feature: Its extraordinary scale as one of the largest monolithic statues in India.
